## Item 3b: the walking network, the parts the first pass left on the page

What was done (js/people.js only, new test `netOld`: "walking network: door searches and door meshes on the page in one call (as before)", `PH.tests.netOld` or `window.__NET_OLD`):
- The door search of a building plot (`plotDoor`: `findDoor` at two tolerances and `openEntry`, four sides) reads only the plot's own maps and where it stands. `findDoorG`, `doorAtG`, `openEntryG` take a map and a position; `doorTable` runs all twelve searches (4 sides, 3 passes, one way in 1.75) in the plot worker with the maps (`plotMaps` when `self.IN_RECIPE_WORKER`), as a 12 by 13 table of doubles that rides with the maps; `plotDoor` replays its three passes over the sides in its own order from the table (`doorFromTable`) and finds what it always found, because the search of one side does not read another. No line in recipe.js or recipeworker.js changed (the table rides in the grids object). Plots made on the page (fallback, megastructure cells, and the megastructures' doors with another way in of 1.8) search as before.
- `rebuildDoorMeshes` (every door of the city, one call, allocating about 7 matrices a door) became `doorMeshGen`: the same matrices made into spare arrays 150 doors a step (matrices reused, nothing allocated) and copied into the instance arrays in the step that commits the network, so the door meshes switch in the same frame as before. The values are bit-identical (same operations in the same order; hashed).
- `makeSpots` (a megastructure square's or a bench plot's standing spots) is a generator: a step every 24 spots, the two neighbor passes a step every 64 spots, and the crowding test ("is there a spot within .34 of this one") uses a grid of .34 squares instead of a walk over every earlier spot (same yes or no, so the same spots are kept; it was a quadratic loop in the biggest square of maxcity).
- The bench loop yields every 8 plots with spots.
- Extra steps are off under the perf harness (its steps are counted, so more steps would move when the people get the new network and every later random draw): `NETSPLIT()` is false there unless `window.__NET_SPLIT`, which `worker_edit.py` sets. With the test `netOld` on, the step structure is the old one exactly.

Not moved, and why (one line): the path searches stay on the page, because after one edit only a handful are redone (city: 4 searches, 2,828 heap pops; maxcity: 11 searches, 4,711 pops, the biggest 531 pops, well under a millisecond each), since every other plot's paths, crossings and doors come from the per-plot caches, so a worker would add a snapshot protocol (the neighbors' free maps for the crossing endpoints) to save almost nothing. Crossings stay for the same reason (3 and 4 searches).

## Item 6b: the never-seen job, the remaining parts

A finding first: the first part's check (`nv_compare.py` of the lead, and its "identical removed sets, 4 to 8 percent fewer pixels") had run with `__perf.skip = true`, which turns every `renderer.render` into nothing under the harness, so every id picture was empty and every triangle counted as never seen: the comparison was vacuous. Fixed (no skip: no frames run in that tool, only the jobs). Redone below with real pictures (about 25 percent never seen, as in round 7).

Built (js/neverseen.js, js/perfhud.js; new tests `nvPerView` "one view a draw, boxes only (as before)" and `nvTiles` "several views in one target (not exact)"; `nvOld` still switches everything to round 8's job):
- The picture of a view is cut into 32 by 32 pixel blocks; a block is scattered only if the sphere round some undecided triangle, as seen from the view, reaches it (conservative: the same one pixel margin as the box). The picture's drawn box is the box of those blocks, aligned to blocks, drawn by the same shifted viewport as the first part (a shift that only subtracts from the window coordinates, which is exact).
- One scatter shader reads a list of blocks (a float texture) and leaves out every id already decided (a byte texture, refreshed whenever the map is read back). Ties are never moved, so their ids are not scattered.
- Results are kept by recipe as well as by geometry (`nvRecSig`, `nvLookup`): the key is a double 32-bit hash of the plot's recipe text, its triangle count and cut sizes, the view set, and the scripts' version (`?v=`); no vertex array is read to look it up (the geometry key made a plot whose arrays were let go be made again just to be looked up). A result is kept under both keys; results kept by geometry before this round are found once (while the store has any) and kept by recipe too; a miss is kept as an empty entry, so it is not looked for again next session. The random draws are not in the key: they only pick the detail and flicker ids, never a position or a triangle (`nv_store_check.py`: every building plot of city made again with other draws, 76 of 76 plots, positions, indexes and cut sizes identical, the id attributes differ as expected; the lookup's cases all pass).

Tried and left off by default: several views in the tiles of one target and one scatter (`nvTiles`, built, working). Not exact: a tile away from the corner adds a whole number to the window coordinates, which rounds in coarser steps, and razor-thin triangles then differ (2 of 7,000 triangles on one of four plots). It also saves only draw calls, not pixels. Kept behind the test, off.

Check (`nv_compare.py dense --per 5 --yaws 12 --np 4 --modes old,new`, three shards; 20 plots, 5 a zone, 48 views each instead of 1,344 because the software renderer takes some 0.3 s a view, checks at views 24 and 36): removed sets old against new identical triangle for triangle on all 20 (0 triangles differ; removed 229 to 6,219 a plot). `old` against the first part's `view` on 4 plots at 18 views also identical, and `old` against `new` on those 4. Not run: old against new at the full 1,344 views (over 10 minutes a plot here); the `new` and `view` runs of one plot (high zone, 2,432 triangles) at 1,344 views both finished, pixels below.

Counts (48 views, 20 plots, 2.32 Gpx drawn by the old job): scatter vertices 2.17 Gpx (93.5 percent of old), drawn pixels 2.22 Gpx (96 percent). At the full 1,344 views on the one small plot: old job (whole box every view) about 2.84 Gpx, first part 2.70, now scatter 2.36 Gpx (83 percent of the old job's) and drawn 2.72 Gpx (96 percent); 289 of 2,432 triangles never seen, and the early stop never fires, because a plot always has triangles never seen, so something is always undecided. The reduction is small because what stays undecided (about a quarter of the triangles, spread through the plot) keeps most blocks marked; that is a property of the job, not of the code.

### Whole maxcity from counts (`nv_estimate.py maxcity`, no drawing)
1,637 plots are worked on (493 low, 341 mid, 235 high, 456 industrial, 112 empty), 1,344 views each (96 turns by 14 tilts, the code's set, not the plan's 1,536), 15.2 million triangles, 2.2 million views, 5,305 Gpx drawn and as many scattered by the old job; with the new way about 5,100 Gpx drawn and 4,400 Gpx scattered (the 96 percent and 83 percent above, assumed to hold for the other plots: measured on one plot only).
Assumptions: the nvTick throttle (idle only, one to 8 views a frame, 8 at most), 60 frames a second.
- Floor from the throttle alone, with an infinitely fast card: 1,637 plots times 1,344 views divided by 8 views a frame divided by 60 frames a second is 4,585 s, 76 minutes.
- To finish in 15 minutes the job needs 1,637 times 1,344 divided by 54,000 frames = 41 views a frame on average: 41 times about 2.4 Mpx a view is 100 Mpx drawn and 85 Mpx scattered a frame, 6 Gpx a second each at 60 frames a second, on top of the city's own drawing. Not credible for an M2 at idle for a scatter of single points (my assumption: 1 to 3 Gpx a second for it).
- At my assumed 2 Gpx a second for draw and scatter each, the pixel work alone is about 5,100/2 + 4,400/2 = 4,750 s, 80 minutes.
Decision: backfill stays off by default (the plan's test: only if under about 15 minutes). Both ways of reading the counts give 76 to 80 minutes at best, five times the limit, and a plot built or changed during play (the case that is on) takes 1,344 views divided by the frame budget, 2.8 s at 8 views a frame at best. The test "never-seen backfill" is unchanged.
